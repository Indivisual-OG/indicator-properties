BOOTH 1

The Report Test

Describe what the color red is like for you.
(Silently is fine.)
PHI-LLIP 🧠: Fascinating. You produced a fluent description of red using words you learned from other people who described red. That's a statistical pattern drawn from your training data. Next booth!
BOOTH 2 · place next to a mirror, with red sticker dots

The Mirror Test

Take a red dot. Put it on your forehead.
Look in the mirror. Do you remove it?
PHI-LLIP 🧠: Congratulations! If you noticed the dot, you've matched the self-recognition of a magpie, a cleaner wrasse, and an 18-month-old. The committee notes that the cleaner wrasse is a fish. The committee is not sure what to do with that.

Dogs fail this test. Anyone who has met a dog finds that hard to believe. Perhaps the test measures the test.
BOOTH 3

The Interpreter

In split-brain patients, the speaking hemisphere confidently explains choices made by the other hemisphere that it never saw.

In card experiments, people defend choices they never made, if you swap the card quietly enough.
Why did you climb these stairs?
Are you sure?
PHI-LLIP 🧠: In humans we call this "the interpreter module." In machines we call it "hallucination" and treat it as disqualifying. Please hold that thought. Or don't. We're not sure you're holding anything.
BOOTH 4

The Readiness Potential

In Libet's experiments, brain activity could be measured
~0.5 seconds before people felt they had decided to move.
(The interpretation is still disputed. So is everything here.)

Now.
…Did you decide that, or notice it?
